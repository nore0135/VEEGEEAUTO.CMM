The fundamentals

A part has a shape.
A CMM gives it coordinates.

Dimensional metrology connects the physical component with the engineering requirements it must meet.

A Coordinate Measuring Machine (CMM) measures points on a component in three-dimensional space. Software uses those points to evaluate planes, holes, circles and surfaces, then compare their geometry with the drawing or CAD model. The result is evidence of how the manufactured part relates to its design.

01 / VERIFY

Quality control

Check dimensions, feature locations and geometric tolerances. Identify deviations before they become assembly problems, rework or rejected parts.

02 / RECONSTRUCT

Reverse engineering

Capture existing geometry to support CAD reconstruction and comparison. A measured part reveals its current shape—not automatically its original design intent or tolerances.

03 / IMPROVE

Manufacturing support

Verify fixtures, support first-piece checks, investigate variation and give production teams clear dimensional feedback.

A practical CMM inspection workflow
  1. Confirm the approved drawing, revision, units and inspection scope.
  2. Prepare and secure the part without introducing unintended distortion.
  3. Verify probe qualification and the equipment’s suitability for the task.
  4. Establish the drawing’s datum or specified reference-point alignment.
  5. Measure features using an appropriate point distribution and strategy.
  6. Review the evaluation settings, tolerances and results before releasing the report.

Measurement quality depends on equipment capability, environment, fixturing, sampling and the evaluation method—not just the displayed decimal places.

The shared definition of a part

An engineering drawing communicates dimensions, tolerances, datum features, material, surface requirements and other specified conditions. Together with the applicable CAD model, specifications and revision controls, it provides the agreed technical basis for manufacturing and acceptance.

  1. Check the part number and drawing revision.
  2. Confirm the units and general requirements.
  3. Identify the datum features and reference scheme.
  4. Read dimensions, GD&T and inspection conditions.
Drawing-reading essentials

Use section views to understand internal geometry. Distinguish basic dimensions, size limits and geometric tolerances. Read the drawing notes and title block before evaluating a feature.

A datum is a theoretically exact reference. A datum feature is the physical feature used to establish that reference. The inspection alignment must respect the specified order and constraints.

Giving every point an address

Three coordinates locate a point relative to an origin and three perpendicular axes. A CMM alignment relates machine coordinates to the required part coordinates so measured features can be compared with the design. The axes do not automatically follow the operator’s view of the part.

ReferenceTypical role in a vehicle gridPractical use
BL · Buttock LineLongitudinal vertical reference planes; commonly associated with cross-vehicle position.Locating lateral features and setting fixture references.
TL · Traverse/Transverse LineStations or sections along the vehicle’s length.Locating longitudinal features and comparing assembly positions.
WL · Water LineHeight relative to a defined horizontal reference plane.Checking feature heights and fixture levels.

Naming and axis conventions vary by manufacturer. BL/TL/WL help define location, alignment and directional deviations; they do not themselves specify a tolerance.

The language of precision

Form. Fit. Function.
Start with the symbol.

GD&T describes permitted variation in shape, orientation and location. Read the whole feature control frame: characteristic, tolerance, modifiers and datum references.

9 controls shown
⌖

Position

Location

Limits departure from a feature’s theoretically exact location under the specified requirements.

Often called true position. Evaluate the appropriate axis or centre plane, datum reference frame and material-condition modifiers. A coordinate difference is not automatically a position result.

▱

Flatness

Form

Keeps a surface between two parallel planes without referencing a datum.

Distribute measurement points across the surface. Sampling only a small area may miss high and low regions. Surface flatness is different from orientation to another surface.

⌓

Profile of a surface

Profile

Defines a tolerance zone around the theoretically exact surface.

The specified datums, zone disposition and other requirements determine the evaluation. Confirm the nominal geometry and alignment before interpreting a deviation map.

⟂

Perpendicularity

Orientation

Controls a feature’s orientation at 90° to a referenced datum.

The tolerance defines a zone, not simply an angular readout. Confirm whether the controlled feature is a surface, axis or centre plane.

∥

Parallelism

Orientation

Controls a feature’s orientation parallel to a referenced datum.

Establish the datum correctly. Parallelism does not by itself define the separation distance between features.

∠

Angularity

Orientation

Controls orientation at a specified basic angle relative to a datum.

Use the basic angle and geometric tolerance together. Do not replace the specified tolerance zone with an arbitrary plus/minus angle limit.

—

Straightness

Form

Controls a line element or, in a different application, a derived feature such as an axis.

Identify exactly what the callout controls. Surface-line straightness and axis straightness require different feature interpretations.

○

Circularity

Form

Controls roundness in individual circular cross-sections without a datum.

Inspect appropriate sections and distribute points around their circumference. Diameter and circularity describe different properties.

⌭

Cylindricity

Form

Controls the overall form of a cylindrical surface without a datum reference.

Sample around and along the cylinder. A single circular section cannot represent the form of the whole cylindrical surface.

Symbols are illustrative learning aids. The drawing’s specified standard and edition govern interpretation and acceptance.

How to read a feature control frame
  1. Identify the geometric characteristic and the feature it controls.
  2. Read the tolerance value, zone shape and any modifiers.
  3. Identify datum references in the specified order, where present.
  4. Check the basic dimensions and applicable drawing notes.
  5. Choose a measurement and evaluation method that implements the complete requirement.

Do not mix rules from different standards or editions without an explicit engineering basis.
